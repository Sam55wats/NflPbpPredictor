NFL play-by-play predictor

What is the model really watching?

The random forests predict whether a pre-snap play becomes a pass, run, punt, or field goal. Feature importance tells us which inputs most often helped the trees split plays into those outcomes.

The lesson in one sentence

The model mostly learns football situation: down, field position, and formation. Among the custom engineered features, red_zone is the only one that behaves like a major signal.

Think of red_zone as a compressed football concept. It says: the offense is close enough to score that normal tendencies change, especially around field goals, goal-line runs, and condensed passing space.

How to read the bars

These are average random forest feature importances across the 32 saved team models. Bigger bars mean the feature contributed more often to useful decision splits inside the trees.

Important caveat: correlated features share credit. For example, red_zone overlaps with yardline_100, and the yardage buckets overlap with ydstogo.

Near the end zone, play-calling changes because the defense has less space to protect, field goals become realistic, and short-yardage decisions carry more scoring leverage. That is why a simple binary flag can punch above its weight.

The yardage buckets are football-smart, but they repeat information the model already gets from ydstogo. A tree can split on ydstogo <= 3 by itself, so short_yardage does not receive all the credit for that idea.

Takeaway

If you improve this model next, start by respecting the big situational signals. Then test new engineered features with permutation importance or ablation, because standard random forest importance can hide value when two features describe the same football concept.
